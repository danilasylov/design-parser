What an old orchard gives that a young one cannot

Six varieties, none of them planted for eating. Bittersweets with more tannin than a young tree will ever carry, picked when they fall rather than when the calendar says.

Windfall, not harvest

We take the fruit the tree lets go. It ripens on the branch a fortnight longer than a picked crop, and it presses sweeter for it.

Four steps, and three of them are waiting

The press runs for eleven days a year. Everything else that matters happens slowly, in a cold barn, without anybody standing over it.

  • I

    Gather

    Fruit is collected off the grass every second morning through November, bruised fruit included — it presses no worse.

  • II

    Mill and rest

    Milled, then left in the tub overnight so the tannin has time to come out of the skins before any pressure is applied.

  • III

    Press

    Slow rack-and-cloth, eleven days in total. Nothing is filtered afterwards; what settles is left to settle on its own.

  • IV

    One winter

    Wild yeast, an unheated barn and no intervention until March. Bottles are released only when a cask stops changing week to week.
