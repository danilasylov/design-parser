Nobody starts knowing

Half the room has never stripped a wire. The first evening is spent making one LED light up, badly, and taking it apart again. By week four you are reading a schematic without asking what the triangle means.

There is no exam and no certificate. There is a shelf at the end with twelve working things on it that were not there in September, and the list of what they are.

How an evening goes

1

Twenty minutes at the board

One idea drawn on the whiteboard: what a capacitor does, why the transistor needs a resistor, what the smoke means. No slides.

2

Two hours at the bench

You build the week's board from a bag of parts and a paper drawing. Two tutors for fourteen people, and neither of them will touch your iron.

3

Twenty minutes of failure

Everything that did not work goes on the table at the end and gets fixed in front of everybody. This is the part people remember.

Fourteen benches

£420 for the twelve weeks, parts included, paid in one go or in three. Four benches each term are free and are given to whoever asks first — no form, no means test, just say so in the letter.

I have never soldered. Is that a problem?It is the assumption. Week one starts with how to hold the iron and what a cold joint looks like.
Do I need to know how to code?Not until week seven, and then only twenty lines. We write them together and they are printed on paper.
What if I miss an evening?The room is open on Thursdays with one tutor. Come and catch up on the board you missed.
Can I bring my own project?Week eleven and week twelve are exactly that. Bring the dead radio, or the drawing you have had for years.
