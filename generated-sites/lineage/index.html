Method What counts

An attribution enters the atlas when three things exist: a room, a period, and a piece of paper. The paper can be a class list, a diploma with a signature, a payroll entry, a letter in which one of them complains about the other. Anything else — a stated admiration, a manifesto co-signed, an obituary claiming a debt — is filed, and stays out of the diagram.

We do not weight the line. A person who ran a two-week workshop and a person who supervised six years of work are connected by the same 1px dashed rule, because we have no honest way to measure the difference and every attempt to invent one has made the atlas worse.

Where a source contradicts another source, both are shown and the entry is marked open. Two hundred and eleven entries are open. They are the most interesting part of the atlas and the reason it is not printed.

A school is not a building. It is a person who taught someone who taught you, and a piece of paper that says they were both there.

Procedure How an entry is made
01Someone writes inTwo names, one room, the years, and a scan. Six hundred and forty of the current entries began as an email from a relative.
02Two readers, separatelyNeither knows what the other decided until both have written a line. If the lines disagree the entry goes to open and stays there until a third document turns up.
03The source is depositedA copy goes to the archive with the entry number written on it. Nothing is in the atlas that we cannot show you within one click.
04The line is drawnDashed, 1px, the same as every other line. Publication happens every other Wednesday, which is the only ceremony we have.
