Approach

Six weeks, three instruments, one clinician

01
A night recorded at home

A courier brings a five-channel recorder on a Tuesday. You sleep in your own bed, in your own noise, and send it back in the morning.

02
Six weeks of ordinary nights

The wearable you already own keeps going. We use it for trend, never for diagnosis — one night is an anecdote, forty is a signal.

03
A person reads all of it

A sleep physician goes through the trace and the diary together, then writes you a plan in sentences. No dashboard, no score, no streak to protect.

What we measure

Five channels, and the reason each one is there

01
Airflow and effort

Separates a pause you stop breathing through from a pause you fight through. The difference decides the whole treatment path.

02
Oxygen saturation

Sampled every second, not averaged per minute. The dips are short by nature and an average erases exactly the thing we are looking for.

03
Body position

For roughly a third of people the whole problem only appears on their back. Position turns an unexplained result into an obvious one.

04
Heart rate variability

Reads the arousals a person never remembers — the ones that shorten deep sleep without ever waking anybody properly.

05
What you write down

Two lines a morning. Coffee, alcohol, the argument at eleven. It is the only channel that explains the other four.
