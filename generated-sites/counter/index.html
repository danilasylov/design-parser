Specimens.

Four families, drawn between 2019 and 2026. Every one of them ships Cyrillic and Latin in the same file, because a face that gets the Cyrillic later is a face that got it wrong.

Ostrov Grotesk

2026 · LAT/CYR/GRK · 9 WEIGHTS · VARIABLE

A newspaper grotesque with the contrast pulled out of it. Drawn for a broadsheet that prints at 8pt on paper thin enough to read the next page through, so the counters are open past the point of comfort.

Аа Бб Вв Гг Дд Aa Bb Cc

Kegel Display

2024 · LAT/CYR · 3 WEIGHTS

Condensed, cut for posters at two metres and nothing smaller. The terminals are flat because a rounded terminal at that size turns into a smudge by the third print run.

Кк Мм Нн Оо Aa Gg

Priyom Mono

2022 · LAT/CYR · 5 WEIGHTS · 600 UPM

A monospace for reading code out loud. The zero has a dot, not a slash, and the Cyrillic Эз and З were redrawn eleven times before an engineer stopped confusing them at eleven at night.

Ээ Зз 0O 1lI Шш Aa

Ligatura Text

2019 · LAT/CYR · 4 WEIGHTS · ITALIC

A book face with a real italic — drawn, not slanted. Commissioned for a publisher of translated poetry, where a line break falls in a different place in every language and the type has to survive all of them.

Фф Цц Чч Ъъ Ыы Aa Ff

Process.

I start on paper at one size, usually 24 point, and I do not move to the screen until the four control letters — н, о, H, O — sit on the same baseline without arguing. Everything else in a family is a consequence of those four.

Cyrillic is drawn in the same pass as Latin, never bolted on afterwards. That decision is the whole difference between a family that reads in Russian and a family that merely contains Russian letters.

Proofing is printed. A face approved on a monitor is a face approved at one size, one weight and one contrast, and it will find you out on the first print run.
