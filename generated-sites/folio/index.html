Collection office

A collection is an asset that has to be looked after twice.

Once as property — insured, stored, catalogued, valued. And once as art, which is the part everyone forgets until it is time to sell.

{ How we work }
What the office does
I

Attribution

Provenance research, condition reports and a written opinion from two specialists who do not know each other's conclusion.

II

Custody

Climate-controlled storage in two buildings, never one, with a quarterly physical count you are welcome to attend.

III

Realisation

When a piece is sold we take a fee on the hammer price and nothing on the valuation — so the valuation stays honest.

Folio
QUARTERLY REPORT

Every three months you receive one bound document: each work, where it physically is, what it is insured for, what changed in the market for comparable pieces, and what we would advise doing about it. Nothing arrives as a dashboard.

Terms of engagement
Minimum
Eight works or a single work valued above twelve million roubles. Below that, an hourly consultation is honestly the better arrangement and we will say so.
Our fee
A flat annual retainer for custody and reporting, and six per cent of the hammer price on anything sold. We never take a percentage of an appraisal.
Conflicts
We do not deal on our own account, we do not hold stock, and we do not accept a commission from the auction house. All three are in the contract.
Leaving
Thirty days, and the collection goes out with its full file — provenance, condition, correspondence — in a form the next office can read.

Tell us what you have inherited.

The first conversation costs nothing and usually ends with us telling you that half of it is worth insuring and half of it is worth keeping for reasons that have nothing to do with money.
