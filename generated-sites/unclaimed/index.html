Getting it back takes three steps and one detail nobody else knows

1

Describe it, then wait a day

Line, date, approximate time, and what the item is. Anything found in the previous twenty-four hours may still be travelling to the depot — most matches happen on the second day, not the first.

2

Name the detail we hid

Every listing leaves something out: what is written on the fob, what is in the left pocket, which sticker is torn. Say it on the phone or in the form and the case is yours without an argument at the counter.

3

Collect, or ask for the post

Bring any identity document to the depot the item is held at. Documents, cards and keys are posted free of charge; anything larger costs what the post office charges us, and nothing more.

What happens to what is never claimed

About sixty per cent of everything found is never asked for. The rules for it were written by the transit authority and are printed at every counter; this is the same text, shorter.

Read the full regulation
  • Ninety daysEverything is held for ninety days from the day it reaches a depot, not from the day it was lost. Documents are held for a year.
  • Perishables, one dayFood and flowers are photographed, listed, and disposed of the same evening. The photograph stays in the case for a year.
  • Phones are not openedLocked devices stay locked. We will not switch one on to find its owner, and we will not let anyone else do it either.
  • After ninety daysItems go to a monthly public sale; clothing and children's things go to two shelters. Proceeds pay for the depots and are published quarterly.
  • Money and jewelleryHeld for a year in the safe at Sadovaya, then transferred to the city budget. Every transfer is listed with its case number.
  • Nothing is thrown away quietlyEvery item that leaves a depot leaves with a line in the register saying where it went, and the register is public.
