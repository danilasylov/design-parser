The build

Four blocks. Every fourth week takes something away.

Volume is written in hours and metres of ascent, not in kilometres — an hour of climbing on scree and an hour on a flat road are different jobs, and only one of them is what you signed up for.

Ascent is the unit

Each week has a metres-of-climb target and a permitted range. Hit it on any hill you can reach: a ski slope, a stairwell, the same 80-metre bank twenty times. The plan does not care which, only that the legs did the work.

Descending is what breaks people

Three weeks of this plan exist only to teach quadriceps to absorb 1 100 metres of downhill without seizing at kilometre 36. It is the least enjoyable block and the one nobody who skipped it finished well.

Every fourth week is smaller

Down weeks are written into the plan as sessions, not as permission. The adaptation happens in them; a runner who trains through four of them arrives at week 13 with nothing left to spend.

Kit

Six things, bought in this order

Everything else can wait until you know whether you like this. The plan tells you which week to start using each item, so nothing is new to you on race morning.

Full mandatory kit list
  • Poles, foldingFrom week 5. They give back about 8 % on a long climb and take two weeks to stop being a nuisance.
  • Shoes with a real lugFrom week 1, on trails. Road shoes on wet scree are how the season ends in week 6.
  • Vest with two soft flasksFrom week 3, worn on every long day, loaded with what you will actually carry.
  • Windproof, seam-sealedMandatory at the start line. Above 2 500 m the weather changes in the time it takes to eat a gel.
  • Head torchFrom week 9 if you finish work at six. Descending in the dark is a separate skill and the plan schedules it.
  • Food you have tested twiceFrom week 7. Sixty grams of carbohydrate an hour, rehearsed on the long day, never invented on the day.
