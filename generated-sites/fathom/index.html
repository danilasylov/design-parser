The method

Apnea is a measurement problem.

Most divers stall because they train sensation instead of data — a good day feels like progress and a bad one like failure. We record the same six numbers every session and let the curve decide when you are allowed to go deeper.

Protocol 04 · revision March 2026

Week 01–02 · Dry tables

CO₂ and O₂ tables at home, twice a day, eleven minutes. Nothing in the water yet.

Week 03 · Ventilation audit

We film your breathe-up and cut it down to four minutes. Most people lose a minute of anxiety here.

Week 04 · Flexibility

Rib-cage and diaphragm work, never past the range the physician marked at intake.

Week 05 · Equalisation

Mouthfill on dry land until it is boring, then the same drill at five metres, head down.

Week 06–07 · The line

Two supervised weekends. Free immersion first, constant weight after, lanyard on both.

Week 08 · Assessment

One measured descent, one static, and a written file you keep — your numbers, not ours.

Required

A medical certificate less than six months old, one previous freediving course of any level, and a two-minute dry static you can repeat on demand.

Not required

A personal best, a monofin, a competition card, or the ability to hold your breath longer than the person next to you.

Grounds to stop

Any squeeze, any blackout, any equalisation failure at depth, or a resting heart rate the physician does not like. The line stops for all four, and the week is repeated.

What you leave with

A file: intake measurements, eight weeks of tables, four session sheets, the final descent profile, and a written plan for the season after.
