One line to wire up

Ping after every run.

Add one curl call to the end of your job. Pulse starts a timer the moment the first ping lands — if the next one doesn't arrive inside your window, the monitor flips, and the on-call chain fires.

# end of nightly-billing-sync.sh
$ curl https://hey.pulse.sh/nightly-billing-sync

{
  "monitor": "nightly-billing-sync",
  "status": "ok",
  "last_ping": "2026-08-26T03:00:04Z",
  "expected_by": "2026-08-27T03:15:00Z",
  "grace": "15m"
}

No agent to install, no SDK to import — a monitor is just a URL. Cron, Sidekiq, Airflow, a Lambda on a schedule: if it can shell out to curl when it finishes, Pulse can watch it.

Grace windows are set per monitor, not globally — a five-minute cleanup job and a four-hour data warehouse sync don't share a threshold, and shouldn't.

Four states. No guessing.

Every monitor is always exactly one of four colors. There's no "checking…" spinner and no ambiguous yellow that means three different things depending on who you ask.

OK412pinged inside its window, right on schedule
Late4grace period running — no page yet, just a flag
Missed3grace expired, no ping — incident opened automatically
Paused9deliberately disabled for maintenance windows
