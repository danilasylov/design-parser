A house is six kinds of block and one crane day

We build timber modules in a shop outside Tver, drive them out on a Tuesday and stand the house in a day. What takes the other four months is the ground, the water and the paperwork — and we do that part with you, in writing.

Parcel: the boring part is the ground

How it actually goes

You come to us with a plot and, usually, with a drawing somebody else made. We do not start from the drawing — we start from the survey, the water table and where the electricity actually comes in, because those three decide more about the house than any elevation.

Then we lay it out from six module types. Not a catalogue of houses: a catalogue of blocks, which you can put together in about forty sensible ways and a few silly ones we will talk you out of.

The shop builds for eleven weeks. On the day, a crane sets the modules on the piles, and by evening the house is closed and dry. Everything after that is inside work you can watch.

Six blocks, one crane day

0118 m²

Living block

Six metres by three, one long window wall, no internal posts. Two of these make the main room.

029 m²

Wet block

Bathroom, boiler and all the pipework, finished and tested in the shop. It arrives working.

0312 m²

Sleeping block

One room, one window, one door, and a wall thick enough that you cannot hear the other one.

046 m²

Entry block

Cold porch, boots, drying rail. In this climate the house starts here or it does not work.

0514 m²

Deck block

Unheated, roofed, and structurally identical to the others, so you can close it in later.

064 m²

Stair block

Only needed if you go up. Half the houses we have built stayed on one floor.

Eleven houses. Two we would build differently.

Both of those had the same fault and it was ours: we put the wet block on the north wall to save pipe, and in February the owners found out why that was cheap.

Parcel: what happens in which week

The whole thing, in weeks

Weeks 1–3 Survey, water table, where the power comes in, and an honest answer about whether this plot should be built on at all. Paid, refundable
Weeks 4–6 Layout from the six blocks. Two rounds of changes are included; the third costs, because by then it is a redesign. Fixed price
Weeks 7–17 Shop build. Photographs every Friday, and you may come and stand in the modules any working day. 60% of the total
Week 18 Piles, crane, house standing and closed by evening. This is the day people take the photograph. Crane day
Weeks 19–24 Inside work, water, heating, and the snagging list you write yourself, item by item, until it is empty. Balance on the list
