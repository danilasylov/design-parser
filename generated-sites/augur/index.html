A forecast that cannot say how sure it is has told you nothing.

01Quantiles, not a single line−
Every step is published as five quantiles — 10, 25, 50, 75, 90. The dispatcher books reserve against the upper one and sells against the lower one; the median is for the report. Models trained on pinball loss keep the intervals honest instead of decorative.
02Weather enters as an ensemble+
Fifty-one perturbed weather members go in, fifty-one load paths come out, and the spread between them is most of the uncertainty you see. When the members disagree about the afternoon cloud cover, the interval widens on its own — no manual inflation factor.
03The calendar is a first-class input+
School holidays, the ten days around New Year, the transfer of working days, the third shift at the two large plants in the zone. Most of the residual error left in this model lives in the four days after a holiday, and we say so on the accuracy page.
04Retrained nightly, evaluated openly+
The window rolls every night at 01:40. Yesterday's forecast and yesterday's meter readings are published side by side the moment settlement data arrives — including the days we were wrong by nine percent.

Eleven inputs, and the meter readings that judge them

  • Weather ensemble51 members, hourly, 2 m temperature, dew point, wind, global radiation
  • Meter readingsSettlement-grade, 15-minute, seven years back
  • CalendarHolidays, transfers, school terms, the local city day
  • Industrial scheduleDeclared shift plans from the eleven largest consumers
  • Price area stateDay-ahead prices and planned outages in the neighbouring zones
  • Distributed generationRooftop solar registry, 41 MW installed, growing monthly
  • Network topologyWhich substation feeds which feeder this week
  • IlluminationSunrise, sunset, cloud opacity — street lighting is a real step
  • Anomaly flagsStorm warnings, emergency shutdowns, mass events in the city
  • Own past errorYesterday's residual by hour, fed back as a feature
  • Settlement correctionsLate meter fixes, so history stops shifting under the model

Runs where the data lives

A container in your perimeter, a read-only connection to the metering database, one outbound call for weather. No consumption data leaves the utility.

Every forecast is kept

Publication time, model version, weather run, input hash. A year later you can reproduce exactly the number the dispatcher saw at six in the morning.

Degrades out loud

When an input stops arriving, the forecast keeps running on a reduced feature set and says so in the header — instead of quietly producing a confident line.
