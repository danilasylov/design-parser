A translated line that does not fit the mouth is not a translated line. We write to timing, cast to the character, and send back the ones that cannot work — before the studio time is booked, not after.

01

Script pass before anyone books a booth

Every string is read against its context: who says it, to whom, over what animation, with how many frames of mouth. Lines that will not fit are rewritten with the writer, in the writer's language, while changing them is still cheap.

Week 1–2 · with your narrative lead
02

Casting you can hear before you agree

Three voices per role, recorded on the actual lines rather than a demo reel, delivered as one file you can drop into the build. You pick, and the actor is held for the whole project — nobody's captain changes voice in act three.

Week 2–3 · 3 options per role
03

Recording with the game running

The director sees the scene, not a spreadsheet. Actors get the previous line in their headphones so a conversation sounds like a conversation, and pick-ups are recorded in the same session instead of three weeks later at a different room tone.

Weeks 4–9 · two rooms, one director
04

Back in the build the same week

Files come named the way your engine wants them, loudness-matched per platform, with a report of every line that changed since the last drop. Integration is our problem until it plays correctly on your machine.

Continuous · Wwise, FMOD, or raw
05

Linguistic QA on the real build

Native players play your game, not a text file: clipped subtitles, a joke that died, an honorific that turned a soldier into a stranger. Each report has a screenshot, a string id, and a suggested fix that fits the box.

Two passes · before and after release

We do not synthesise voices.

Not because a model cannot read a line — it can, and cheaply. Because a performance is a decision made by a person who understood what the scene was about, and because the actor whose voice trained the model did not agree to be in your game.

We use models for the boring half: aligning takes to timecode, catching a clipped consonant, spotting the four hundred strings that changed since the last build. Nothing that speaks is generated.

Send us a hundred lines and the build. We will send back three voices and an honest schedule.
