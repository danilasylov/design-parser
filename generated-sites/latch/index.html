Latch

Migrations that show their locks.

Shipping a schema change used to mean guessing: which rows go under lock, for how long, and whether the deploy will hold the table while ten thousand requests queue up behind it.

Latch reads the statement, replays it against last night's snapshot, and prints the lock it will take — before the deploy, not during the incident.

What this migration will hold, and for how long

Replayed against a 41 GB snapshot restored at 03:12. Times are measured on that copy, not estimated by the planner.

# Statement Lock Holds Rows
1 CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY idx_orders_created … ShareUpdateExclusive 2 m 14 s8.4 M
2 ALTER TABLE orders ADD COLUMN paid_at timestamptz AccessExclusive 4 ms—
3 UPDATE orders SET paid_at = created_at WHERE … RowExclusive 6 m 02 s, batched8.4 M
4 ALTER TABLE orders ALTER COLUMN paid_at SET NOT NULL AccessExclusive 1.9 s8.4 M
5 DROP INDEX idx_orders_created_old AccessExclusive 12 ms—

Statement 4 is the one that costs you: at 14:00 traffic, 1.9 s of AccessExclusive queues about 320 requests. At 04:00 it queues eleven.

Four things it does before you merge

Replay on a copy

Restores last night's snapshot, runs the migration against it, reports the real duration instead of the planner's guess.

Lock queue preview

Shows which queries would pile up behind the statement, and how deep the queue gets at the traffic you actually have.

A rollback that exists

Every generated migration ships with its down-script, and the check refuses to pass without one that has been run at least once.

A window, not a night

Reads four weeks of traffic and marks the hours when an exclusive lock costs the fewest queued requests. Usually not 3 a.m.
