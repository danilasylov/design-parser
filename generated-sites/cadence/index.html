What you get

A scheduler that keeps its receipts

Every guarantee below is observable from the API — not a promise in a status page.

Deterministic retries

Backoff is computed from the first attempt, not from when your worker woke up. The same failure always produces the same retry schedule.

Timezone-correct schedules

Schedules are stored with their zone, not converted to UTC. Daylight saving shifts move the job — they do not silently skip or double-fire it.

Replay any run

Every execution keeps its exact payload for ninety days. Replay it against staging, or against the same endpoint, with one call.

The run log

Not “it failed”. What failed

Every tick is a row you can open: the payload it carried, the status your endpoint returned, how long it took, and which attempt finally stuck.

  • Request and response bodies stored verbatim for ninety days
  • Drift measured per tick, not averaged over the hour
  • Alerts fire on the attempt that failed, not on the last one

Use cases

Things teams stop writing twice

Digests, invoices and end-of-day reports

The classic nightly job, except the finance team can see which invoices went out at 02:00 and which one returned a 500 — without opening your logs.

Data pulls from suppliers who wake up late

Set a window instead of a minute. Cadence keeps trying inside it with the backoff you defined, and stops the moment one attempt succeeds.

Trials, dunning and anything with a deadline

One-shot schedules created from your own code, cancellable by the same key. No sweeper job scanning a table every five minutes.

Cache warming and index rebuilds

Fan out one tick into a thousand jobs with a concurrency ceiling, and see the whole fan-out as a single run in the log.
