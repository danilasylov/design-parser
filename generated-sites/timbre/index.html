Signals

Six sounds that arrive before a stop

None of them are audible from the walkway. All of them repeat on a schedule the microphone can count.

Bearing spall

A rolling element chips. The chip answers once per revolution — a click buried ten decibels under the motor.

6–9 weeks

Pump cavitation

Vapour bubbles collapse against the impeller. It sounds like gravel in a bucket, and it eats the blade edge first.

3 weeks

Belt slip

A squeal that shows up only under load, and only in the first minute of a shift — right when nobody is standing there.

10 days

Gear mesh wear

Tooth contact loses its rhythm. The mesh frequency grows sidebands that no operator would ever name out loud.

5 weeks

Rotor imbalance

One thump per turn, louder by a fraction of a decibel each week, until the coupling finally answers for it.

2 weeks

Loose anchor bolts

The frame replies to the motor half a beat late, and the floor slab starts carrying sound it was never meant to carry.

4 weeks
Platform

One console for every line

01

The listener

A sealed microphone on a magnet, powered over Ethernet. Nothing is wired into the machine, nothing touches the PLC, nothing needs recertifying.

02

The model

Trained on the first fourteen days of your own line. A bearing in Kaluga does not sound like the same bearing in Turin, and we stopped pretending otherwise.

03

The console

One page per line: a drift bar, a date, and the ten seconds of audio that made the call. There is no dashboard to assemble.
