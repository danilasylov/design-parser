The house

A keeper's house, kept

Built in 1911 for the two families who tended the light, empty from 1998, and opened again in 2022 with the same floor plan, the same stove positions and the same view of a sea that freezes for eleven weeks a year. Nothing was moved to make room for a lobby, because there is no lobby.

The table

One dinner, at eight, for everyone staying

There is no menu and no à la carte. The cook drives to the harbour at six, buys what came in, and puts it on one long table at eight. Vegetarians are asked when they book, allergies the week before, and nobody is asked to choose between two fishes that were caught the same morning.

The light

Still a working navigation aid

The tower is not ours and never will be — it belongs to the hydrographic service, and a technician comes twice a year to turn the lens by hand and check the seal. Guests may climb it on Saturdays, six at a time, with him if he is there and with us if he is not.

December – February
Ice to the horizon, dinner at four, the beam visible for eleven miles.
March – May
The ice goes out in a week. Migrating geese, and the boathouse opens.
June – August
Light until midnight, so the lamp barely earns its keep. Booked earliest.
September – November
Storms from the south-west, and the best evenings of the year indoors.
