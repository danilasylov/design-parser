A shelf is cheaper than a shop

A carpet cleaner is used for roughly forty minutes a year. A pressure washer, twice a summer. A projector, once. Every one of them sits in a cupboard on a street where thirty households own the same thing and none of them can find the manual.

A library of things is the ordinary answer. One shelf, one catalogue, one set of keys held by people who live on that street. You borrow it, you bring it back, and the next person finds it clean.

Every library is run by its own members and owns what stands on its shelves. Mutual writes the rules nobody wants to write twice — insurance, deposits, opening hours, what to do when something breaks — and then gets out of the way.

How borrowing works

All the rules
01

Join in four minutes

One form, one neighbour who vouches for you, no fee and no card. Membership is for a person, not a household — everyone signs their own name.

02

Reserve what you need

The catalogue shows the shelf, not a warehouse: if it says the tile cutter is in, it is in, and it is a two-minute walk from where you are standing.

03

Borrow it for a week

A week is the default and nobody chases you on day eight. Deposits exist only for the six most expensive items, and they are returned in full.

04

Break it, and we fix it together

Nothing is charged for ordinary wear. Bring the broken thing to Saturday's repair café and you will leave knowing why it failed.

Your street already owns enough.

Thirty households, one shelf, and a set of keys. We will send you the founding kit — the rules, the insurance template and the catalogue — and a member from the nearest library to sit through your first meeting.
