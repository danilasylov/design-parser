How it works

Three parties, one ledger, no surprises

01

The studio issues

Items are minted against the studio's own supply rules. We never create an item and we cannot change a drop rate — the game stays the game.

02

The vault holds

Your items sit in a vault keyed to you, not to an account on a server. Losing your login is a support ticket; losing the studio is not your problem.

03

The market clears

Sales settle in forty seconds and the studio takes its royalty in the same transaction, which is the only reason studios agreed to any of this.

When a game closes

Four hundred servers went dark last year. Items in eleven of those games survived.

A closed game is not a hack and not a scandal — it is a Tuesday. What Panel changes is what is left afterwards: a signed record of what you held, a vault that still opens, and a market where somebody who still cares can buy it.

Fees

Written down, all of them

01 Holding an item in a vault. Costs us nothing, so it costs you nothing, and that will not quietly change next year. Free
02 Selling on the market. Taken from the sale, never from the balance, and split with the studio in the same transaction. 2.5%
03 Cashing out to a bank card, including the payment provider's cut, which we publish in the receipt rather than burying it. 1.1%
04 Moving an item out to a wallet you control elsewhere. We would rather you stayed, but the door is not locked. Free
For studios

You keep the economy. We keep the receipts.

Panel does not touch drop rates, supply or pricing — those stay in your build. What you get is a royalty that pays on every resale for as long as the item exists, and a promise you can make to players that costs you nothing to keep.
