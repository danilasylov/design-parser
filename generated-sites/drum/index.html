What comes off the drum

Overprint.

A risograph lays down one ink per pass. Two passes make a third colour where the layers cross, and that overprint is the only honest reason to print this way instead of sending the file to a laser printer down the street. Everything else about the machine — the misregistration, the roller marks, the way a solid never comes out solid — is what the file has to be drawn around.

We print posters, zines, election broadsheets, seed packets and one wedding invitation a year. Runs from 25 to 3 000 sheets. Below 25 it is cheaper for you to photocopy it, and we will say so.

What we need from you
  • One greyscale file per ink, named by ink
  • 300 dpi, no rich blacks, no transparency left live
  • 3 mm trim, and nothing critical inside 8 mm of the edge
  • Registration drift of about 1 mm designed into the overlaps
What you get back
  • A wet proof of every ink pair before the run
  • Two spare sheets per hundred, at no charge
  • The file back, flattened the way it actually printed
  • Roller marks. They are part of the price, not a defect
