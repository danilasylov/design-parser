The life of one tube

1.Intake

The courier's bag is opened in front of the screen. Client, matrix, sampling time, temperature on arrival, who handed it over. A barcode is printed and stuck on before the tube leaves the counter — that is the only moment the sample exists without a label, and it lasts about nine seconds.

Holding time starts from the sampling time, not from the moment you typed it in.
2.Aliquoting

One tube becomes four: metals, anions, organics, retain. Each child keeps a pointer to its parent, so a doubtful result three weeks later can be traced back to the same bottle, the same courier, the same afternoon.

The retain aliquot is created automatically and cannot be discarded before the report is signed.
3.Analysis

The instrument writes its own file; Aliquot reads it and attaches the raw data to the aliquot, not to a folder on somebody's desktop. Calibration, blank, control sample and their acceptance limits are stored with the run, so a batch that failed its control cannot quietly produce results.

Supported out of the box: Agilent, Shimadzu, Thermo, Khromos, and any instrument that can write CSV to a folder.
4.Verification

A second person sees the number, the control, and the deviation log side by side, and either signs or sends it back with a reason. Sending back is one click and is recorded — a lab where nothing is ever returned is a lab where nobody checks.

Two signatures are required by default; the second cannot be the person who ran the analysis.
5.Report

The protocol is built from the same records, with method, uncertainty, and limits printed next to every figure. A client who asks what the number means gets the answer on the same page instead of a phone call.

PDF and a signed XML, plus a link that expires in ninety days.

Chain of custody, as it is actually kept

Every touch is a line: who, when, what changed, and the hash of the record before it. Nothing is edited in place — a correction is a new line with the reason typed by a human.

Holding time that argues back

Counted from the sampling moment and shown in hours left, not in a colour. When it runs out, the sample cannot be moved into analysis without a written deviation.

Reagents with a shelf life

Lot, opened date, expiry, and which runs used it. When a standard turns out to be bad, you get the list of affected results in one query instead of a week of paper.

Works when the network does not

The intake screen and the label printer keep working offline and reconcile afterwards. Laboratories are full of basements, and a courier will not wait for your Wi-Fi.
