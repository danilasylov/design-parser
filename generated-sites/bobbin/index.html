Tape nobody was allowed to copy.
The label Radio tape, 1964–1991

We buy the tape, not the rights

Provincial radio committees recorded everything and broadcast most of it once. When the buildings were sold the tape went into basements, and about a third of it is still playable if you handle it correctly and only once.

We find the reels, bake them, transfer them at 38 cm/s, and then spend far longer finding whoever played on them — or their children — before anything is released.

Released

The rest are transferred and waiting: we could not find the musicians, or we found them and they said no.

50%
Goes back

Half of everything a release earns goes to the players or their heirs, from the first rouble, before we take costs.

Tape Because it was made for tape

Pressed to cassette, in runs of three hundred

Every release goes out as a chrome cassette with the transfer sheet printed inside the J-card: reel number, speed, machine, the date of the broadcast and the name of the engineer who recorded it, where the box still said.

The same master is free to stream. We are not precious about it — the tape is for the people who want the object, and the object should tell you where it came from.
